\subsection{Archive identity and conditional compilation replay}
The historical K562 fold-0 benchmark contains $3{,}125$ requests: $349$ retained comparative terminals and $2{,}776$ nonidentifiable terminals. The field-audited query table has $3{,}125$ rows, $12$ columns, unique \texttt{query\_id} values and one fold identity; its SHA256 is \texttt{\seqsplit{31d1a33b84c49df325ef3480fe4ddbb400293c4ff819de03f94100f674502db8}}. Conditional replay infers TRAIN membership from the archived Q1 identities and uses \texttt{consensus\_effect} with OLS to reproduce every saved terminal label and all $349$ retained readouts, with maximum absolute discrepancy approximately $2.3\times10^{-14}$.

Cryptographic identity and conditional numerical agreement serve different purposes from source-bound native compilation. In this reconstruction, the original sealed TRAIN mask, producer call lineage and complete per-query source maps remain unbound. Thus the replay verifies arithmetic under the recovered reference; it does not independently reissue the original source certificates. In prospective compilation, the record binds $(X_R,Y_R,F_R,R_R,R_E)$ and the local witness at emission time.

\subsection{Effective resistance and witness-norm diagnostics}
Assume independent TRAIN noise with covariance $\sigma^2 I$ and minimum-norm OLS completion $F_R=X_R^+$. For any estimable query, the reference-adjustment witness $h^\top=a^\top X_EX_R^+$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reff-s}
\operatorname{Var}(h^\top Y_R)=\sigma^2\|h\|_2^2
=\sigma^2a^\top X_E(X_R^\top X_R)^+X_E^\top a.
\end{equation}
For a signed bipartite incidence design and a two-node contrast, this coefficient is the classical effective resistance. An independent equal-variance TEST observation adds its own variance; for an atomic readout the total coefficient becomes $1+R_{\mathrm{eff}}$. These interpretations depend on the declared noise model, whereas the witness identity itself is algebraic.

For the $129{,}000$ archived atomic TEST instances, both original and one-bridge graphs make every request estimable, but the median reference-variance coefficient rises from $0.07862$ to $1.21967$; the median per-mask ratio is $15.53$. This is a modeled variance coefficient. At equal $20\%$ acceptance coverage, R1-to-R2 directional disagreement is $18.965\%$ for magnitude, $18.740\%$ for magnitude divided by $\sqrt{1+R_{\mathrm{eff}}}$, and $18.767\%$ for an event-degree proxy. At $50\%$ coverage the rates are $25.969\%$, $25.862\%$ and $25.864\%$. Witness norms therefore supply a precision diagnostic under the noise assumptions, while the archived selection gains over a degree proxy are modest.

\section{Four-cell repeat-direction reliability and selection dependence}
\label{supp:q4}
\subsection{Overlap dependence in rectangle enumeration}
Let the rows of $A$ be balanced four-cell coefficient vectors. The readout covariance is $A\Sigma_YA^\top$. Under isotropic independent cell noise, two rectangles sharing one cell have coefficient inner product $\pm1$ and rectangles sharing two same-row or same-column cells have inner product $\pm2$, so reused observations induce covariance. On a complete $R\times E$ panel the rectangle space has dimension $(R-1)(E-1)$, which may be much smaller than the number of enumerated rectangles.

The processed K562 table contains $12{,}898$ observed pairs, $32$ regulators and $1{,}140$ events, with $496$ regulator pairs. Exhaustive enumeration gives $20{,}978{,}446$ overlapping rectangles. Across the two stored channels, $12{,}813{,}931$ nonzero cases agree in direction ($61.08\%$), $8{,}164{,}504$ disagree ($38.92\%$), and $11$ involve a zero. The equally weighted regulator-pair agreement is $66.46\%$. A separate Kendall inversion/counting implementation reproduces the arithmetic.

\subsection{Effective sample size and selective inference}
The TEST rectangles reuse cells, events and regulators. Event-disjoint selection within regulator pairs retains $14{,}831$ contrasts but still shares information across pairs. Thus rectangle counts are structural exposure counts, and independent-trial confidence bounds do not follow from them. The recorded accepted-error rates do not establish a $90\%$ selection-conditional correctness guarantee.

\section{Source-ledger refinement, nonlinear rescaling, and two-GEM dual nuisances}
\label{supp:sources}
\subsection{Refinement stability of the algebraic source gate}
As summarized in Corollary~1 of the main text, let $R^{\mathrm{coarse}}=R^{\mathrm{fine}}C$ use the same ordered observations and completion rule, and let $J(R)=R_E-X_EF_RR_R$. Then $J(R^{\mathrm{coarse}})=J(R^{\mathrm{fine}})C$, so
\[
\mathcal L(R^{\mathrm{fine}})\subseteq\mathcal L(R^{\mathrm{coarse}}).
\]
Fine-ledger cancellation therefore implies cancellation under every admitted aggregation. If the identity aggregation is allowed, it is also necessary for cancellation across all such versions. A coarse grouping can hide cancellation between distinct source atoms that a refinement exposes.

For a declared fine-source shift $\|\delta u\|_2\le\rho$, duality of the Euclidean norm gives the exact sensitivity radius
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-radius}
\sup_{\|\delta u\|_2\le\rho}|a^\top J(R^{\mathrm{fine}})\delta u|
=\rho\|a^\top J(R^{\mathrm{fine}})\|_2.
\end{equation}
Alternatively, with independent bounds $|s_j|\le\rho$, $|\eta_i|\le\varepsilon_i$ and $\lambda=(-a^\top X_EF_R,a^\top)$, the deterministic readout radius is $B=\rho\|a^\top J_0\|_1+\sum_i|\lambda_i|\varepsilon_i$. The interval $[z-B,z+B]$ certifies a strict direction only when it excludes zero. These bounded-source statements do not replace the exact cancellation criterion or constitute confidence intervals.

\subsection{Why group-dependent standardization changes native four-cell contrasts}
As an algebraic illustration, suppose the two regulator rows of a four-cell query are standardized with different positive scales $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$. Within-row centering cancels, but
\begin{align*}
a^\top\widetilde Y_E
&=\frac{Y_{r_1e_1}-Y_{r_1e_2}}{\sigma_1}
-\frac{Y_{r_2e_1}-Y_{r_2e_2}}{\sigma_2}\\
&=\left(\frac1{\sigma_1}-\frac1{\sigma_2}\right)(\beta_{e_1}-\beta_{e_2})
+\frac{\Gamma_{r_1e_1}-\Gamma_{r_1e_2}}{\sigma_1}
-\frac{\Gamma_{r_2e_1}-\Gamma_{r_2e_2}}{\sigma_2}+\widetilde\varepsilon.
\end{align*}
Unequal scales change the contrast and can retain additive event variation. This derivation is an illustration, not an additional Replogle expression-level experiment.

For the archived GEM-by-gene standardization $z_{bpg}=(x_{bpg}-\mu_{bg})/\sigma_{bg}$, the corresponding original-scale grouped contrast is
\[
\Psi(\sigma)=\sum_bw_b[\sigma_{bg_1}d_{bg_1}-\sigma_{bg_2}d_{bg_2}],
\quad d_{bg}=z_{bp_1g}-z_{bp_2g}.
\]
If positive scales vary independently, all-zero coefficients give a tie, nonzero coefficients of one sign give a stable direction, and mixed signs permit opposite directions under admissible scales. Two standardized differences equal to one give original-scale contrasts $+1$ and $-1$ under scales $(2,1)$ and $(1,2)$. Three justified same-group, same-gene anchors can define an affine-invariant ratio, but that is a different quantitative object. The raw-scale V3 query definition retains its original measurement operator.

\subsection{Native-identity two-GEM stress configurations}
The metadata program forms $128$ eligible two-GEM pools from $310{,}385$ cell identities, $48$ GEM groups, $2{,}176$ targeted guides, $97$ non-targeting guides and $8{,}563$ genes. This study uses identities and source topology; it does not read the approximately $10$-GB H5AD expression matrix or estimate new biological amplitudes.

For GEM-by-gene (BG) and GEM-by-perturbation (BP) nuisance, order the cells as $(p_1g_1,p_1g_2,p_2g_1,p_2g_2)$ with coefficients $(1,-1,-1,1)$. With one GEM per cell and direct cancellation without reference compensation, BG requires matching GEMs along each gene pair, while BP requires matching GEMs along each perturbation pair. The rectangle propagates these equalities to all four cells. Consequently no genuinely cross-GEM assignment among the $16$ two-GEM placements cancels both families directly. Two complete within-GEM rectangles give an eight-cell construction with a two-dimensional direct cancellation space. A new reference compensation $h$ requires its own bound $(X_R,F_R)$ check.

The archived median shared-control sampling-variance fraction of $2.4466\%$ assumes independent homoskedastic cell noise. For a declared two-GEM heterogeneity bound $|\theta_1-\theta_2|\le H$ and variance proxies $\sigma_\Delta^2u_b$, the classical minimax weighting objective has solution
\[
\frac{\mathrm{MSE}_{\max}(w)}{\sigma_\Delta^2}=w^2u_1+(1-w)^2u_2+h^2(w-\tfrac12)^2,
\quad w^*=\frac{u_2+h^2/2}{u_1+u_2+h^2},\quad h=H/\sigma_\Delta.
\]
This conditional weighting calculation is separate from measured expression effects.

\section{Decision-only information, bounded domains, and exact LP controls}
\label{supp:decision}
\subsection{Unrestricted and bounded completion domains}
For fixed predictions and common $W\succeq0$, write $s=U^\top W(p_B-p_A)$ and $\Delta(t)=\Delta(0)+2s^\top t$. With unrestricted $t$, a zero slope gives a constant difference and a nonzero slope attains both strict orders. Domain knowledge may instead declare a nonempty compact polytope $\mathcal T=\{t:Ct\le d\}$~\cite{vanwaarde2020informativity,bennouna2025decision,ye2026decisionsufficient}. A strict A preference is certified when $\max_{t\in\mathcal T}\Delta(t)<0$; a strict B preference when $\min_{t\in\mathcal T}\Delta(t)>0$. An identically zero range is a certified tie. Other zero-containing ranges defer a strict preference and may include a one-sided tie boundary.

\subsection{Box-constrained extrema and LP winner sets}
For $\mathcal B_\infty(\tau)=\{t:\|t\|_\infty\le\tau\}$, norm duality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polytope-s}
\begin{aligned}
\max_{t\in\mathcal B_\infty(\tau)}\Delta(t)&=\Delta(0)+2\tau\|s\|_1,\\
\min_{t\in\mathcal B_\infty(\tau)}\Delta(t)&=\Delta(0)-2\tau\|s\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
When $s\ne0$, the first radius admitting a tie is $\tau^\star=|\Delta(0)|/(2\|s\|_1)$; the opposite strict order is attainable beyond that boundary. With $s=0$, the original difference is constant at every radius. Testing whether a model can attain simultaneous minimum loss on a polytope reduces to LP feasibility for all inequalities $\Delta_{m,m'}(t)\le0$ with one shared $t$. The comparator must use simultaneous constraints rather than only independent pairwise range tests~\cite{boyd2004convex,kossen2021activetesting}.

The archived matched synthetic fixture contains $89$ models: $1$ always rejected, $42$ accepted and $46$ ambiguous. PAIR uses $43$ LP calls; the independently constructed classical LP comparator uses $42$, with identical states. This fixture establishes agreement with the exact decision formulation, not a solver-call or runtime advantage. When $W=Q$, $QU=0$ makes the truth-only pairwise difference constant on every bounded domain as well.
